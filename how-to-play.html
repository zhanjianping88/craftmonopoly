Beginner Guide

How to play Craft Monopoly

Craft Monopoly is easy to learn. Choose your builder, set up a local table of 2–10 people and AI rivals, then race to claim land, upgrade your favorite biome, and outlast the table in a quick match.

Start a match Read strategy tips

1. Start your table

  1. Open the homepage and choose the number of players.
  2. Set each slot to human or AI.
  3. Choose a skin for each player.
  4. Launch the match and wait for the first turn prompt.

2. Take your turn

Each turn starts with a two-dice roll. Your builder moves across 28 spaces and resolves the landing right away: collect a bonus, buy or upgrade property, pay rent or tax, or draw a random event card.

3. Buy smart properties

When you land on an unowned property, you can buy it. Control at least two of the three plots in a biome to earn its monopoly rent bonus.

4. Understand the main tile types

Property tiles

Buy them when available, then use them to collect rent from other players.

Tax tiles

These take money from you, so cash management matters.

Chance cards

These can swing momentum with money rewards, money losses, or a short warp across the board. Nightfall is a separate tile that costs your next turn.

Jail

Nightfall can cost you your next turn. Use the extra breathing room to plan your next property move.

Start and rest-style bonus tiles

Some spaces give you a cash reset or small advantage that helps you stay in the match.

5. Win condition

The World Storm starts after the setup rounds. Each active turn costs upkeep that rises every round. End your turn below zero and you go bankrupt. The last builder standing wins.

FAQ

How many players can join?

You can set up 2 to 10 local players, mixing pass-and-play humans with AI builders.

Can I play alone?

Yes. Fill the other slots with AI players and start a solo strategy run.

What happens if I run out of money?

If your balance drops below zero, you go bankrupt and leave the table. Keep a coin buffer before upgrading.

Common beginner mistake

Buying too aggressively and running out of liquid cash leaves you exposed to taxes, rent spikes, and bad chance pulls.
